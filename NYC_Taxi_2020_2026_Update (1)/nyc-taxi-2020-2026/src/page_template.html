About the data

Which trips appear in the dashboard?

Card and cash trips are included when the base fare and total charge are above zero, and the pickup date falls within the month covered by the source file. Trips that do not meet these conditions are left out.

Each record counts as one trip. The same passenger can take several trips. These records cover green taxis only.

What do the money columns mean?

Base fares: the trip price before separately listed tips, taxes, tolls and extra charges.

Passenger charges: the reported total paid for the trip, including recorded extras. Cash tips are not included.

Average base fare: total base fares divided by the number of included trips.

Recorded tips: tips listed in the data; cash tips are missing.

Fares per calendar day: total base fares divided by the number of days covered. These amounts are not profit because business expenses have not been subtracted.

How will this become a stronger portfolio project?

The next step is to investigate why fares and trip counts changed. Comparing the same months across years can help. Information about fare changes, weather or major events could provide context, but a chart alone cannot prove the cause.

How are the years and locations compared?

2020–2025 contain all 12 months. 2026 contains January–August only. All years combines these 80 months. Use January–August in the annual chart to compare matching periods.

Pickup zones show where trips started. Drop-off zones show where they ended. Both charts rank locations by base fares from the same trips, so their totals should not be added together.

Amounts are shown in the dollars recorded at the time, without an inflation adjustment. Very high fares and repeated records are not automatically removed.
